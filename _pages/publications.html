Research

Research Interests

I study the mass politics of international security, seeking to understand how war and political violence reshape public opinion, state–society relations, and group boundaries within societies. My research is organized around three streams. The first examines how the unequal distribution of wartime burdens across social groups and regions shapes support for leaders and war, and reshapes the boundaries between social groups. The second studies how exposure to political violence and its memory shape foreign policy and security attitudes. The third examines how domestic group identities — partisan, ethnic, and national — condition political responses to international conflict.
